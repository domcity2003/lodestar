# v1 six-degree-of-freedom model: trim, turbulence, kit release, motor-out, launch and belly landing

2026-11-15

## Answer

The frozen v1 flies well in the 6-DOF model, with one exception: the 14 m/s kit-drop run. Five findings call for a design or operating change (listed at the end, with proposals).

- **Trim is easy everywhere.** Across 1.1 × stall to VD, kit on and off, at sea level and 2,000 m, trim needs at most 2.3° of elevon. That is 15% of the 15° pitch budget. Level flight at VD (33 m/s TAS at 2,000 m) needs 69% of continuous thrust.
- **The search case has margin, even in severe turbulence.** At 20 m/s, moderate turbulence gives a P95 bank of 5.4° and a lowest airspeed of 1.17 × stall (P5). Severe turbulence gives 1.04 × stall (P5) and 4.4% elevon saturation. No run stays above CLmax for more than 0.25 s.
- **The 14 m/s drop run has no stall margin.** For the 8.3 kg v1, 14 m/s is 1.11 × stall. In light turbulence, 49 of 50 runs hold CL above CLmax for more than 0.25 s. Moderate turbulence at the 18 m/s rule speed still gives 10 of 50. At 20 m/s it gives none.
- **The kit release itself is benign.** At 14 m/s in calm air, the autopilot gains 0.8 m and loses 0.16 m, with a pitch transient of 2.5°. With the controls frozen at the release, the aircraft climbs 7 m in a phugoid and never loses height.
- **Motor-out glide is 17.8:1, sinking 0.99 m/s at 17.75 m/s.** That is kit on at 2,000 m. Kit off, it is 17.6:1 at 16 m/s, sinking 0.91 m/s. The transition from 20 m/s takes a few seconds, never goes below 17.3 m/s, and a 30° banked glide turn needs 12° of aileron. The glide polar is in `out/sixdof_v1/glide_polar.csv`.
- **The bungee launch at 16 m/s holds 1.1 × stall only if the motor is at full power at release.** At 4,400 m, the margin is 1.13 × stall. A 0.5 s motor-start delay drops the speed to 1.08 × stall, and a 1 s delay to 1.06 ×. The 30° crosswind case (12 m/s wind, 22° sideslip at release) is controllable: 4.8° peak bank, and the aircraft weathervanes 26° into the wind.
- **The belly landing is gentler than the FE design case in calm air, but not in light turbulence.** With the flare, touchdown sink is 0.35–0.42 m/s; without it, 0.80–0.92 m/s. In light turbulence, the P95 sink is 1.58 m/s and the worst of 25 runs is 1.81 m/s, against the FE design sink of 1.5 m/s.
- **The 40 mm skid stroke is only reached if the skid is soft enough.** At 1.5 m/s, the skid must absorb 7.5 J (kit off) or 9.4 J (kit on). The CAD v4 skid footprint (300 × 25 mm) in the softest EPP crushes only 10–12 mm, at 9–11 g. That matches the 10 mm hard-ground case in which the FE model failed the root.

## Model

`scripts/sixdof_v1.py` writes `out/sixdof_v1/aircraft/lodestar_v1/lodestar_v1.xml` for [JSBSim](https://github.com/JSBSim-Team/jsbsim) 1.3.3 and runs every case below. Every input is read from an existing source; none is re-entered by hand. The v2/v3 model in `out/jsbsim/` is unchanged.

| Lift, pitching moment, lateral derivatives | Tables in α from 10 trimmed AVL runs, CL −0.1 to 1.0 | `controls_sizing.avl()` (v1 planform, pod, winglets; moment reference at the kit-on CG) |
| Drag | NeuralFoil MH45 profile drag × halfway finish factor + CL²/(π·0.8·7) + pod and gimbal area | `propulsion_margins.drag()`, which is also the mission-sim model |
| Maximum thrust vs airspeed | 22–27 N at 15–20 m/s | `propulsion_margins.available()`: CAM 15×10, AT3520-550, SiCore 6S5P at 15 °C (30 s limits at launch) |
| Elevon travel and servo | ±20° geometric per surface; 250°/s surface rate; 20/s lag | `lodestar_v1.CONTROLS`, plain-flap effectiveness from `controls_sizing.effective()` |

The roll and yaw derivatives from AVL are stability-axis derivatives. The model forms the moments in stability axes and rotates them to body axes. The lift is linear, so the model has no stall break. A run counts as a **stall** if CL stays above CLmax 0.80 (`lodestar_v1.OPERATIONS`) for more than 0.25 s, the rule the 2026-11-11 study used. This is conservative: the AVL strip analysis puts first-strip stall at a 3D CL of about 0.9–1.0.

**Autopilot.** A simple ArduPilot-class loop runs at 50 Hz:
- airspeed sets thrust (PI);
- altitude sets pitch (PID), and pitch sets elevon (PI plus pitch-rate damping);
- bank hold sets aileron (P plus roll-rate damping);
- gains are scheduled with (V_trim/V)²;
- glide and approach use pitch-for-airspeed;
- the flare holds sink rate.

The elevon/aileron mixing clips each surface at 20° geometric, and that clip is what "elevon saturation" counts.

**Turbulence** is JSBSim's MIL-F-8785C Dryden model ([MIL-F-8785C](http://everyspec.com/MIL-SPECS/MIL-SPECS-MIL-F/MIL-F-8785C_5295/)), low-altitude form. The wind at 20 ft is 15 / 30 / 45 kt for light / moderate / severe. JSBSim chooses the low-altitude form from the height above sea level. So the turbulence runs put the terrain at sea level and warm the air until its density equals 2,000 m, as the 2026-11-11 study did.

**Cross-check with the wing FEA** (`research/2026-11-15-wing-fea.md`, `out/fea/landing_cases.csv`). The FE landing case is a half-sine pulse at 1.5 m/s. With 40 mm of stroke, it peaks at 4.5 g, and every margin is +0.7 or better. With a 10 mm stroke the pulse peaks at 18 g, and the root skin fails (−0.64).

The CAD v4 footprint in EPP of any density stops the pod in 3–12 mm at 9–33 g. The skid easily has the energy (30–87 J available against 9.4 J needed), but it is far too stiff to give the pulse the FE model assumed. **The stroke requirement alone does not protect the root; the crush force must also be specified.**

For both kit states to use at most 40 mm and stay at or below the FE model's 4.5 g peak, the footprint must be:
- **23–29 cm² of EPP 20 g/L**; or
- **14–18 cm² of EPP 30 g/L**;
- and at least 57 mm thick, so that 40 mm is 70% strain.

A tapered (wedge or V) section that grows its contact area with stroke is the usual way to get this.

**Tip clearance.** In a belly landing, the wing-tip trailing edge is the next point down after the skid (`tip_clearance.csv`).
- **Flare pitch 6.5°, CAD 4 mm skid:** the tip trailing edge clears by only 22 mm wings level, so 0.9° of bank puts it on the ground first.
- **57 mm EPP skid:** 2.9° of bank does.
- **Pitch 4° with the EPP skid:** the allowable bank grows to 4.2°.

The light-turbulence touchdowns have a P95 bank of 2.9°. Washout lifts the tip trailing edge about 10 mm; this is not counted.

## Findings that require a design change (proposals; `lodestar_v1.py` is not changed)

1. **Drop-run speed.** Raise the release run-in from 14 m/s to at least 18 m/s in calm or light air, and to 20 m/s in moderate turbulence. In `OPERATIONS`, that means changing `turbulence_min_tas` from 18 to 20 for the drop run. At 2,000 m, 14 m/s is 1.11 × stall for the 8.3 kg v1, below the 1.25 × rule. 16 m/s still stalls 7 runs in 50 in light turbulence. The 18 m/s light case was not run. The drop-accuracy study (`research/2026-11-12-drop-accuracy.md`) used 14 m/s, so it should be rerun at 18–20 m/s. The faster run-in adds forward throw, which the release-point solution has to absorb.
2. **Launch motor start.** Add an operating rule: at density altitudes above 3,000 m, the motor reaches full power at release (`TKOFF_THR_DELAY` ≈ 0, with the prop clear of the launcher by design). The alternative is a 16.5 m/s release minimum. With a 1 s motor delay, 16 m/s gives 1.06 × stall at 4,400 m.
3. **Belly-skid specification.** Specify the skid by crush force as well as stroke:
   - a 23–29 cm² EPP 20 g/L contact area, or a tapered section reaching that by 40 mm of crush;
   - at least 57 mm thick;
   - mean crush force 190–295 N.

   Also update CAD v4: its skid is still the 4 mm, 300 × 25 mm strip, and that footprint in any EPP is several times too stiff.
4. **Landing design sink.** The light-turbulence P95 touchdown sink (1.58 m/s, max 1.81) exceeds the FE design value of 1.5 m/s. Rerun the FE landing margins at 2.0 m/s with the specified skid. `out/fea/landing_cases.csv` already holds 2.13 m/s loads, which roughly double the root moment of the 1.5 m/s case. An alternative is to soften the flare in turbulence; the calm no-flare touchdown is only 0.8–0.9 m/s.
5. **Flare pitch and tip protection.** Limit the flare pitch to about 4–5°, since the calm no-flare sink is already 0.8–0.9 m/s. Fit replaceable wear pads under the wing-tip trailing edge and winglet roots. At the 6.5° flare pitch, the tip touches before the skid above 0.9–2.9° of bank.

## Limits of this model

- **Lift and stall.** The lift is linear in α, with no stall break and no dynamic-stall delay beyond the 0.25 s rule. The stall counts are an indicator, not a departure prediction.
- **Ground effect** is not modelled. It lowers the sink near the ground, so the landing sinks here are conservative.
- **Folding prop.** It is assumed to fold with zero drag. A windmilling prop would steepen the glide.
- **Turbulence and wind.** There is no wind shear or terrain-induced downdraft (the 2026-11-11 stress test covered long downdrafts). Turbulence is the frozen-field Dryden model with no rotational gusts beyond JSBSim's.
- **Launch.** The rail angle, cradle incidence and launcher efficiency are assumptions.
- **CG.** The model flies the actual v1 CG. The CG-envelope extremes are covered by `scripts/controls_sizing.py`.
