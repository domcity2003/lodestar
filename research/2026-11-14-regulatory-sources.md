# Regulatory and standards sources for a civil SAR UAS

2026-11-14

Not legal advice. Status is as of 2026-11-14; several items come from trade press and law-firm summaries, flagged where so.

## Plain summary

Lodestar (6-8 kg, under 55 lb) fits Part 107 today. Beyond-visual-line-of-sight flight still needs a waiver or COA because Part 108 is not final. Remote ID is required. Dropping a rescue kit is legal only if it does not create an undue hazard. For the defense-adjacent angle, a Blue UAS-listed or domestic parts list is the strongest design signal, because the FCC now blocks new authorizations for foreign-made drone components.

## Current status (late 2026)

- Part 107: in force. Limits above apply; BVLOS needs a waiver under the waiver framework or a COA ([status guide](https://www.flyusi.org/guides/get-108-ready?hsLang=en-us)).
- Part 108: still a proposal. NPRM 2025-08-07; about 3,100 comments; right-of-way and electronic conspicuity comments reopened January-February 2026; final rule at OIRA since 2026-07-10, no publication date ([guide](https://www.flyusi.org/guides/get-108-ready?hsLang=en-us)). The 240-day deadline in the executive order passed on 2026-02-01 ([Pick Drones summary](https://pickdrones.com/faa-part-108-bvlos-rule-delayed-oira-2026/)).
- Proposed Part 108 shape: permits for lower-risk operation types (a "civic interest" type exists) and certificates for riskier ones; most permit types cap aircraft at 55 or 110 lb; manufacturers declare compliance to an FAA-accepted consensus standard; FAA airworthiness acceptance with at least 150 flight hours of reliability testing; automated flight with no handheld manual control; detect-and-avoid, lost-link action, flight data recorder and Remote ID; manufacturers limited to U.S. or bilateral-agreement countries ([guide](https://www.flyusi.org/guides/get-108-ready?hsLang=en-us)). Everything here may change in the final rule.
- Public safety: agencies flying as public aircraft keep their COAs. Civic-interest permits would serve civil operators contracted to government ([guide](https://www.flyusi.org/guides/get-108-ready?hsLang=en-us)). The proposed foreign-manufacturer restriction (Section 108.700) was flagged by commenters as significant for first responders ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-om/insights/publications/2025/10/faa-proposed-part-108-bvlos-rule)).
- Remote ID (Part 89): the discretionary enforcement period ended 2024-03-16 per trade sources ([DroneDesk](https://blog.dronedesk.io/faa-remote-id-rule-explained-for-commercial-pilots/)). I did not verify the ">0.55 lb" threshold or exact public-safety exemptions against the eCFR; check [Part 89 on eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-F/part-89/subpart-B).
- FCC Covered List: foreign-produced UAS and critical components added 2025-12-22; new equipment authorizations blocked, existing models not immediately barred ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/fcc-adds-all-foreign-made-drones)). Since 2026-01-07, items on the Blue UAS list or qualifying as Buy American domestic end products are exempt ([Akin Gump via Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/terrorism-homeland-security-defence/1732524/fcc-excludes-certain-foreign-produced-uas-and-uas-critical-components-from-covered-list-and-provides-guidance-for-dod-and-dhs-conditional-approvals)). Litigation over the Covered List was reported in 2026 ([Holland & Knight, May 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/citing-national-security-needs-the-fcc-and-faa-take-steps-on-uas)).
- Blue UAS: list now run by DCMA; over 39 systems and 165 components as of December 2025; Green UAS certified platforms are added automatically ([DroneLife](https://dronelife.com/2025/07/17/diu-expands-blue-suas-list-to-include-green-suas-platforms-clarifies-certification-pathways/), [DIU](https://www.diu.mil/blue-uas-cleared-list)).
- Not verified: NDAA FY2025 section numbers beyond those named in the FCC notice, and any FAA "declaration of compliance categories" beyond the Remote ID declaration process.

## Design implications for Lodestar

1. Keep takeoff mass under 55 lb (it is about 8 kg, about 18 lb) and cruise under 87 kt; both hold with large margin ([107.3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/107.3), [107.51](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/107.51)).
2. Specify a Remote ID solution: a built-in standard Remote ID unit or a broadcast module with an FAA-accepted declaration of compliance, referencing ASTM F3586-22 and F3411 ([FAA MOC notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2022-08-11/html/2022-16997.htm)). Plan antenna placement and power budget.
3. Drop-kit design: document the drop envelope, release altitude and airspeed, descent device (consider a parachute per ASTM F3322) and a no-drop-over-people rule so each release meets "no undue hazard" ([107.23](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/107.23)).
4. Treat BVLOS as a future mode: design for lost-link behavior, a flight data recorder, detect-and-avoid provision and automated flight, which the Part 108 proposal lists ([guide](https://www.flyusi.org/guides/get-108-ready?hsLang=en-us)).
5. Build a parts list from Blue UAS-listed components or U.S.-made ones (flight controller, radios, camera, batteries, motors) to stay clear of the Covered List ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/fcc-adds-all-foreign-made-drones)). The thermal camera and radio are the likely hard items.
6. Show program-grade rigor by tailoring a MIL-HDBK-516C-style criteria matrix and mapping design and test evidence to ASTM F3298 ([tailoring thesis](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/handle/10919/98018), [F3298-24](https://store.astm.org/f3298-24.html)). Label it a tailored reference, since the handbook is guidance only.
7. State the operator type in the write-up: volunteer SAR groups use Part 107; government agencies may use a public COA ([FAA FAQ](https://www.faa.gov/uas/public_safety_gov/public_safety_toolkit/Public_Aircraft-Public_Safety_Operation_CoW-COA_FAQ.pdf)).
